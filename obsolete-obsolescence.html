Obsolete Obsolescence

What if a machine becomes obsolete twice? A discarded VHS player reborn as a robot that still hopes to be useful.

The Story

At the UW Surplus store I found a lavender machine with a rose-gold front panel and a broken, empty interior, and bought it for about three dollars, thinking it was a CD player. My instructor looked at it and asked, "Are you sure this isn't a VHS machine?" I had never seen one.

That moment changed the project. I read about VHS and the video-rental shops that once connected communities, and kept thinking of Wall-E: a small machine still doing its job after the world that needed it has moved on. So I made the VHS player a "sentient" robot. Alone, it runs a pointless routine. When someone approaches, it waves, makes noise to get their attention, and proudly presents something it considers advanced: a CD. The CD is itself obsolete, hence the name.

A last-minute decision turned out to be the best one: in about 20 minutes I added sound, a working hum and a "ta-da" when it presents the CD. In a dim, busy gallery, the audio is what made people stop and look.

Context

Solo project for DXARTS 471: Mechatronic Art, Design and Fabrication, UW

Timeline

Jan – Mar 2024 (11 weeks)

Built with

Salvaged VHS mechanism, servos, DC motor, motion sensor, Arduino, speaker

Role

Everything: concept, mechanics, electronics, code, sound

The Build

I reused the original tape-tray mechanism for the robot's main movement. Servos drive a waving "hand" and a mock reading head, and a hidden motor pushes the tray out to present the CD, whose iridescent surface catches the light.

What I Learned

I found I'm more comfortable expressing ideas through experimental art than I expected, and I loved it. This project started my path toward reflective and speculative design research, and it's personally one of my most important projects.
